What we do

Three programs. One anonymous entry point.

Every part of what we build follows the same rule: a student should never have to identify themselves, ask permission, or risk consequences to stay safe.

Program 1

Free & anonymous access

Resource stations placed in nurse's offices, libraries, and other everyday spaces — not gated behind a pharmacy counter or a conversation with an adult.

  • Naloxone (nasal spray), free and ready to use
  • Multi-drug test strips: fentanyl, xylazine, nitazenes, and benzodiazepines
  • Plain-language instructions and a QR code to anonymous educational resources
  • No sign-up, no ID, no name attached to any pickup
Program 2

The Ambassador Program

A small, consistent team at every partner school that keeps the program running and connects students to resources.

  • 1–3 student ambassadors, 1–2 faculty members, and one administrator per school
  • Monthly meetings within each school; bimonthly meetings across the district
  • Biweekly Friday newsletters sharing educational resources, volunteer opportunities, and where to find drug safety resources
  • Ambassadors track program progress and flag what students actually need
Program 3

Looking ahead

These are in active development. They're part of the plan, not yet part of the program — we'd rather build one thing well than announce three things at once.

In development

Nicotine patch access

Connecting students under 21 to existing prescription pathways for nicotine replacement therapy, so quitting doesn't require navigating the process alone.

In development

Safe e-waste disposal

A simple directory of where to responsibly dispose of vapes and other electronic waste around Oakland and the Bay Area.

In development

Legal protection for youth

Advocating for the same overdose amnesty protections California recently extended to public colleges — but for K–12 students, too.
